## 1. Forensic Dissection Overview

In Stage 8.9.19, two models established the project's Pareto frontiers:
- **Model C (Multi-Scale Routing):** Highest recall (**92.31%**, 72/78 screams) and perfect short-scream recall (**100%**), but high shouting FPR (**21.43%**).
- **Model E (Neural Attention Fusion):** Lowest hard-negative FPR (**2.60%**) and shouting FPR (**8.33%**), but low recall (**56.41%**, 44/78 screams).

Stage 8.9.20 conducted an exact case-by-case error dissection across all 462 held-out test files to determine why their decisions diverge.

**Model C is a strict empirical superset of Model E on genuine screams.**

## 4. Synthesis Pathway for Model Architecture

This forensic discovery proves that Model C and Model E do not possess complementary, orthogonal knowledge; rather, **Model E is a conservative, highly regularized sub-manifold of Model C**.
- Directly combining Model C and Model E probabilities via averaging simply pulls the decision threshold between the two extremes, degrading both advantages.
- The correct synthesis pathway (realized in Stage 8.9.20 Model E) is **Error-Aware Conditioning**:
  - Keep Model C's short-window onset router for brief signals ($\le 0.75$s) or pitch failure cases to preserve 100% short-scream recall.
  - Apply LPC formant dispersion ($F_2 - F_1$) and pitch jitter filters specifically on sustained vocalizations ($>0.75$s) to weed out the 28 sustained shouting false alarms.
