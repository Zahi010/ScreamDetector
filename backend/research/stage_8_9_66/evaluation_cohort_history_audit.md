## 3. Discrepancy & Prevalence Drift Forensics

### A. Why did the allocation shift from 240/810 to 550/500?
In Stage 8.9.64 and 8.9.65, research discovered that Candidate E1's primary historical vulnerability was short screams (<1.00s).
To achieve statistical power for fine-grained subgroup comparisons (e.g. ultra-short $\le 0.50$s vs standard short), the Stage 65 planning team inflated positive allocations to 550.

### B. Flaws in the Balanced 550/500 Design
1. **Prevalence Distortion:** A 52.38% scream prevalence is unrepresentative of operational environments where emergency events are rare anomalies ($<1\%$).
2. **Under-Sampling Hard Negatives:** Compressing negatives to 500 constrained the budget for shouted speech (only 120 allocated) and industrial noise.
3. **Precision Distortion:** A balanced cohort produces an artificially high positive predictive value (PPV), masking false alarm costs.

### C. Why Reconciled 350/700 is the Authoritative Solution
- **$N=350$ Positives:** Provides 220 short screams and 130 sustained screams. As proven in Track F power calculations, 220 short screams provides **$>99.5\%$ statistical power** for Gate 4 ($\ge 90\%$), eliminating the need for 550 positives.
- **$N=700$ Negatives:** Provides 160 shouted-speech files (powering Gate 3 to $>96\%$) and 540 diverse environmental/transient negatives (powering Gate 2 to $>98\%$).
- **Realistic 1:2 Ratio:** Balances statistical efficiency with operational rigor.
