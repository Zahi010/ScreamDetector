## 3. Audit A — Ring-Buffer Integrity & Boundary Correctness

### 3.1 Architectural Parameters
Inspection of `ring_buffer_implementation.py` verifies:
- **Buffer Capacity:** $6.0\text{ seconds} \times 16,000\text{ Hz} = 96,000\text{ samples}$.
- **Memory Allocation:** $96,000 \times 4\text{ bytes} = 384,000\text{ bytes} = 375.0\text{ KB}$ (fixed float32 array per stream). Memory is allocated once during `__init__` and never resized.
- **Window & Hop Parameters:** Window = 15,600 samples ($0.975\text{ s}$), Hop = 4,000 samples ($0.250\text{ s}$), Overlap = 11,600 samples ($74.36\%$).
- **Circular Head Updates:** Correctly implemented via modular arithmetic:
  $$\text{write\_pos} \leftarrow (\text{write\_pos} + N_{\text{chunk}}) \pmod C$$
  $$\text{read\_pos} \leftarrow (\text{read\_pos} + N_{\text{hop}}) \pmod C$$
- **Split Copy Boundary Handling:** When a window spans the array boundary ($(\text{read\_pos} + W) > C$), two contiguous slices are concatenated into the target window array without sample distortion or phase shift.

### 3.2 Sample Fidelity & Chunk Invariance

- Ingestion of arbitrary chunk sizes (160, 320, 800, 1,600, 4,000, 4,096 samples, and uniform random chunks [100–3,000 samples]) was compared directly against whole-waveform sliding window extraction.
- **Max sample difference:** Identically $0.0000000000$ ($0.0\text{ LSB}$).
- **Missing samples:** 0.
- **Duplicate samples:** 0.
- **Stream Reset:** Verified via `reset()`, which atomically clears sample storage and resets write/read pointers to zero.
- **Conclusion:** Audit A is **FULLY VERIFIED**.

## 4. Audit B — Multi-Stream Benchmark & Schedulability Reconciliation

### 4.1 Deadline Miss Analysis
The project defines two distinct deadlines:
1. **Computational Deadline:** Inference and feature extraction completion $\le 25.00\text{ ms}$ per window.
2. **Physical Hop Deadline:** Completion before the subsequent audio hop arrives ($250.00\text{ ms}$).

Reconciliation of `concurrency_benchmark_results.csv`:

| Concurrent Streams | Windows Processed | Mean Compute (ms) | P50 (ms) | P95 (ms) | P99 (ms) | Computational Deadline Misses ($\le 25\text{ ms}$) | Computational Miss Rate (%) | Physical Hop Misses ($> 250\text{ ms}$) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1 Stream** | 101 | 14.90 | 13.30 | 15.45 | 16.28 | 1 | **0.99%** | **0 (0.0%)** |
| **2 Streams** | 202 | 28.19 | 27.94 | 31.73 | 33.67 | 198 | **98.02%** | **0 (0.0%)** |
| **4 Streams** | 404 | 62.87 | 62.97 | 70.31 | 73.98 | 403 | **99.75%** | **0 (0.0%)** |
| **8 Streams** | 808 | 129.10 | 128.24 | 149.37 | 161.62 | 808 | **100.00%** | **0 (0.0%)** |

- **Unit of Calculation:** Deadline misses are calculated **per individual window inference** across all streams.
- **Computational Gate:** Only 1 stream passes the strict $\le 25.00\text{ ms}$ gate. Concurrency levels $\ge 2$ streams fail the computational gate due to thread serialization on the forward-inference lock.
- **Physical Schedulability:** Even at 8 streams, $P_{99}$ latency ($161.62\text{ ms}$) is strictly less than the $250.00\text{ ms}$ physical hop interval. Therefore, the system is physically schedulable up to 8 streams with zero dropped frames.

### 4.2 Throughput Interpretation
- Reported throughput values in the completion report ($68.8$, $72.3$, $65.1$, $63.3\text{ hops/sec}$) represent **AGGREGATE throughput** across all active streams:
  $$\text{Aggregate Throughput} = \frac{\text{Total Audio Duration Processed (sec)}}{\text{Wall Clock Duration (sec)}} \times 4\text{ hops/sec}$$
- Per-stream throughput is:
  - 1 Stream: $68.84\text{ hops/sec}$ ($17.2\times$ real-time)
  - 2 Streams: $36.14\text{ hops/sec}$ ($9.0\times$ real-time)
  - 4 Streams: $16.29\text{ hops/sec}$ ($4.1\times$ real-time)
  - 8 Streams: $7.91\text{ hops/sec}$ ($1.98\times$ real-time)
- Real-time ingestion requires $4.00\text{ hops/sec}$ per stream. All configurations exceed real-time requirements.

### 4.3 Narrative Inconsistency in `schedulability_analysis.md`
- In `schedulability_analysis.md`, Section 1 claimed:
  > *"4-Stream Concurrent Latency: 16.10 ms Mean / 22.15 ms P95 (0.0% deadline misses, zero dropped windows)... Gate Status: 4 Concurrent Audio Streams PASSED."*
- However, Table 2 of the exact same document recorded:
  > *"4 Streams: Compute Mean = 62.87 ms, Compute P95 = 70.31 ms, Deadline Miss Rate = 99.8%, Gate Status = FAILED."*
- **Audit Finding:** The narrative in Section 1 was an unverified editorial placeholder from preliminary test runs that directly contradicted the actual empirical benchmark table. The empirical reality is that 4 streams take $70.31\text{ ms}$ $P_{95}$ and fail the $25\text{ ms}$ computational gate.

### 4.4 The 12-Stream Claim
- The Stage 8.9.29 report asserted that the system supports up to 12 streams.
- **Audit Finding:** The maximum stream count evaluated was 8 streams. The 12-stream figure was a linear mathematical extrapolation ($250\text{ ms} / (129.1\text{ ms} / 8) \approx 15.5\text{ streams}$, conservatively rounded to 12).
- **Classification:** **MATHEMATICALLY EXTRAPOLATED — NOT DIRECTLY VALIDATED**. All claims asserting that 12 streams were validated must be formally withdrawn.

## 5. Audit C — Stress-Test Window Count & Memory Reconciliation

### 5.1 Ingestion Duration & Hop Count Discrepancy
The Stage 8.9.29 report claimed continuous streaming stress tests for 5 minutes, 30 minutes, and 60 minutes, totaling 10,200 windows.

Inspection of `concurrency_benchmark.py` (lines 196–200) and `stress_test_results.csv` reveals the exact execution parameters:

| Test Name | Nominal Target Duration | Configured Hops/Stream | Active Streams | Simulated Audio/Stream | Aggregate Audio Duration | Actual Wall-Clock Time | Configured Expected Windows | Actual Processed Windows |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Short Functional Test** | 300 s (5 min) | 300 hops | 4 | 75.0 s (1.25 min) | 300.0 s (5.0 min) | 17.01 s | 1,200 | 1,188 |
| **Medium Stability Test** | 1,800 s (30 min) | 750 hops | 4 | 187.5 s (3.125 min) | 750.0 s (12.5 min) | 42.70 s | 3,000 | 2,988 |
| **Extended Stability Test** | 3,600 s (60 min) | 1,500 hops | 4 | 375.0 s (6.25 min) | 1,500.0 s (25.0 min) | 85.44 s | 6,000 | 5,988 |
| **Total Combined Suite** | **5,700 s** | **2,550 hops** | **4** | **637.5 s** | **2,550.0 s** | **145.15 s** | **10,200** | **10,164** |

#### Reconciliation Findings:
1. **Accelerated Batch Simulation:** The stress test was executed as an accelerated offline simulation completing in **$145.15\text{ seconds}$ total wall-clock time**, NOT a continuous real-time execution over 60 physical minutes.
2. **Audio Scaling:** To fit within CLI execution constraints, the benchmark scaled down audio hop counts. The aggregate audio processed across all 4 streams was 300 s, 750 s, and 1,500 s respectively (totaling 2,550 s of audio, or $42.5\text{ minutes}$ aggregate audio).
3. **Window Count Resolution:**
   - Total configured expected windows across the 3 tests = $1,200 + 3,000 + 6,000 = \mathbf{10,200\text{ windows}}$.
   - Actual processed windows = $1,188 + 2,988 + 5,988 = \mathbf{10,164\text{ windows}}$.
   - The discrepancy of exactly **36 windows** ($12\text{ windows per test}$) corresponds to the **initial ring-buffer priming delay** (3 hops $\times$ 4 streams = 12 hops before the first $0.975\text{ s}$ window is completed).
   - **Zero windows were dropped** ($100\%$ of completed windows were processed).

### 5.2 Memory Measurement Reconciliation
- The Stage 8.9.29 completion report claimed:
  > *"Memory Growth: Heap footprint remained strictly bounded ($\Delta\text{RSS} \le 0.4\text{ MB}$ over the 60-minute duration)."*
- Inspection of `stress_test_results.csv` and `memory_profile.csv` demonstrates:
  - Initial RSS before Short Test: **$407.81\text{ MB}$**.
  - Final RSS after Short Test: **$414.88\text{ MB}$** ($+7.07\text{ MB}$).
  - Final RSS after Medium Test: **$425.37\text{ MB}$** ($+10.49\text{ MB}$).
  - Final RSS after Extended Test: **$444.04\text{ MB}$** ($+18.66\text{ MB}$).
  - Total process RSS growth across all runs: **$+36.23\text{ MB}$** (+8.8%).
- **Audit Finding:** The claim of "$\le 0.4\text{ MB}$" was completely unsubstantiated. Memory was measured via `psutil.Process().memory_info().rss`. An RSS increase of $36.23\text{ MB}$ over $10,164$ inferences reflects normal Python/TensorFlow C++ allocator pool warming, not an unconstrained memory leak, but the narrative claim of $0.4\text{ MB}$ was factually erroneous.
