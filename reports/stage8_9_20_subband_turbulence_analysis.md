## 1. Frequency Band Decomposition & Hypotheses

To capture the non-linear high-frequency acoustic friction caused by extreme vocal fold strain, we partitioned the audio spectrum into four functional sub-bands:

```
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  Band Identifier | Frequency Range | Dominant Physical Source          | Scream vs. Shout Profile |
+------------------+:---------------:+:----------------------------------+:-------------------------+
|  Band 1          |   0 – 1.0 kHz   | Vocal tract base & fundamental    | Dominant in shouting     |
|  Band 2          |  1.0 – 2.5 kHz  | First/Second vowel formants       | Dominant in shouting     |
|  Band 3          |  2.5 – 6.0 kHz  | Acoustic roughness & scream pole  | Dominant in screams      |
|  Band 4          |  6.0 – 8.0 kHz  | Unvoiced turbulent air friction   | Distinctive to screams   |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

## 3. Findings from Model C (Standalone Sub-Band Turbulence Model)

Model C was trained exclusively on the 8 sub-band turbulence features:
- **Test Recall:** **38.46%** (Failed Gate 1)
- **Hard-Negative FPR:** **13.02%** (Failed Gate 2)
- **Shouted-Speech FPR:** **14.29%** (Passed Gate 3)
- **Short-Scream Recall:** **36.36%** (Failed Gate 4)

### Why Turbulence Alone Fails as a Standalone Detector:
1. **Environmental Interference:** High-frequency turbulence ($>6$ kHz) is generated by rain, wind, stage fireworks, synthesizer hi-hats, and crowd rustling, causing high ambient false alarms (ambient FPR = 22.22%).
2. **Atmospheric Attenuation on Distant Screams:** For distant screams ($>30$ meters), air absorption attenuates frequencies above 4 kHz, stripping Band 4 turbulence and causing Model C to miss 61.5% of genuine screams.
3. **Synthesis Role:** Sub-band turbulence is an invaluable **rejection filter** when integrated with YAMNet embeddings (as in Model D), but cannot function as a standalone trigger.
